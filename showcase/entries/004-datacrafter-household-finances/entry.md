Open banking apps read a household's accounts and transactions in the Consumer Data Right
shape and copy them into a spreadsheet. That shows today. This showcase keeps every day: each
day's transactions are a dated snapshot of their own, so the history and the trend come for
free, and one report shows this month's budget, today's net position and the last 120 days.

The household, the Harpers, and their banks, Coral Bank and Southern Mutual, are fictional.
Every transaction is generated by DataCrafter in the Consumer Data Standards banking shape:
accounts, balances and transactions with the standard's own field names and codes.

The data comes from a Data Generator, and its rules do the work. Scheduled rows put the
calendar in place: the two salaries land every second Wednesday and Thursday, the home loan
repayment on the 1st, the savings transfer on the 15th and the card repayment on the 20th, the
bills on their days, and fees and interest on the last day of the month. A direct debit due on
a weekend moves to the Monday. Each transfer has both legs on the same day, money out of the
everyday account and into the other one. The first picture shows the result: the everyday
account fills on each payday and empties as the bills go out.

Everything else is drawn. One combination rule draws a whole spending pattern per row,
weighted by how often the household does it: a grocery shop always comes from the everyday
account with the supermarket's merchant category code, a coffee always from the card. A Normal
range varies each amount around its pattern's typical value, a formula turns the two into the
amount, a sequence numbers the day's transactions, and the row count varies so some days are
busy and some are quiet. The seed is fixed per date, so running a day again gives exactly the
same rows, on any server.

A Classifier sorts every transaction into a category group and a category, merchant category
code first, the way a budgeting app's rules do, but as a model the rest of the solution reads.
Balances are never generated: each one is the opening balance plus every transaction up to that
day, and figures computed outside DataCrafter match every number in the report.

The charts are real Excel charts inside the report's workbook. The first three pictures are the
run of 13 November, downloaded from the Demo server and opened in Microsoft Excel. The last two
show the same run in DataCrafter's web client. Each morning at 7 the Scheduler generates the new
day and stores the day's report.
